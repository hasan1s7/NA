# Proposed equal-time human pilot

**Status: proposal only.** No recruitment, participant contact, registration, approval, randomization, reminders, data collection or study launch has occurred. The design below is one concrete next study, not evidence that Nabu improves learning. Its 12-minute session and 48-hour delay are design choices to test, not estimated optimal durations. No sample size is asserted.

## Question and narrow task

Does offering decision-sensitive help or an optional diagnostic improve a learner's later unassisted performance on a specific language task, compared with a fixed support sequence under the same available time?

The proposed setting is a reviewed Arabic dialogue and a narrowly defined practical purpose: understand and use a considerate request in a short written exchange. Participants choose whether this activity is relevant to them before assignment. The study does not evaluate general Arabic proficiency or assume a single culturally correct form of warmth or respect.

Use a small content pool reviewed by an Arabic-language expert before the pilot. The current eight authored contrasts are software fixtures and candidate materials, not already validated study instruments. Review must establish intended readings, acceptable variants, register boundaries, source provenance and eligibility of every morphological application. It must correct or exclude the existing quarantined poem mapping. Freeze the reviewed content, rubrics, scorer version and lexical-family manifest before collecting results.

## Assignment and equal time

Use participant-level random assignment to two parallel arms. Parallel assignment avoids a learner receiving both policies on the same forms. Any baseline stratification variables and allocation process must be specified in advance; do not adapt assignment to make one policy appear better.

Both arms receive the same initial instructions, reviewed media, available support actions, direct-evidence model, candidate pool and **12 minutes of wall-clock activity**. A brief baseline assessment is identical in both arms and is recorded separately from treatment. Its time is also held equal, and its possible practice effect is acknowledged. The task clock begins at the same defined boundary in both arms.

| Arm | What differs | What remains equal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed support | Offers a predeclared sequence of task-relevant checks and explanations, regardless of earlier correctness | Reader, content pool, task, rubric, observation model, response affordances and total available time |
| Decision-sensitive support | Uses the frozen transparent rule to choose direct help, a short diagnostic whose outcomes change the next help, or continuation | Same materials, assistance choices, maximum time and assessment opportunities |

Both arms retain **skip**, **help now** and **continue reading**. Assignment governs which option is offered next; it does not remove the learner's ability to decline. Record offered and selected actions separately. A click is a selection, never a successful learning outcome. No arm receives extra material, extra session time, a stronger learner model or a larger pool disguised as a policy improvement.

Record actual time spent, unused support time, reading time, question count, help actions and returns to media. Equal available wall-clock time does not imply equal numbers of practice questions or equal time spent practicing; those are policy consequences to report, not hidden matching variables.

## Immediate and delayed assessment

At the common session endpoint, administer the same short task-specific assessment to both arms under a frozen rubric. This assessment has its own time allowance and does not extend one treatment session selectively. Report comprehension and production separately, with actual modality and assistance conditions.

The primary delayed assessment is scheduled for **48 hours after the session**, with a proposed predeclared **42–54-hour window (±6 hours)**. Record actual elapsed time and report deviations rather than treating an appointment label as a measured delay. It is **unassisted**: no source text, translation, answer, hint or playback is available during the response. If assistance is requested or exposed, retain the event and report its assisted condition separately; do not count it as the primary unassisted outcome.

Use a reviewed, genuinely different sentence frame and communicative situation for the practiced phrase. Record which syntax, context, speaker or modality changed and which remained the same. A new question ID is insufficient. A second probe can test independently specified new lexical families **only if reviewed eligible material and independently supported base meanings are available**. Keep this separate from a within-family probe. Supplying a new base's meaning would make that probe assisted; it must not be relabelled as an unassisted test of meaning or broad transfer.

The existing exact-answer software matcher is too narrow to establish all acceptable human production. Have blinded scorers apply a preregistered task rubric to bounded responses, with allowable variants, disagreement handling and an audit sample defined before analysis. Keep the scorer's version and the original response so a correction can be replayed without becoming another learner attempt.

## Outcomes and analysis

The proposed primary outcome is the proportion of delayed unassisted responses meeting the prespecified task-specific rubric. Immediate completion of the defined communicative task is a secondary outcome. Freeze the reviewed criteria and aggregation before collection; do not select a favorable endpoint afterward. Report immediate assistance effects separately from delayed results.

Secondary outcomes include question count, action-level interruptions, elapsed time, return-to-media behavior, voluntary abandonment, perceived disruption, and within-family versus independently specified new-family performance when those probes exist. Keep recognition, retrieval and contextual production separate. Report opportunities for exposure as opportunities, without assuming attention.

Use participant assignment as the comparison unit and account for repeated items and lexical-family grouping in the planned analysis. Report uncertainty, effect-size estimates and missingness by arm. A skipped or missing delayed response is not an incorrect answer. Preserve the randomized-assignment comparison where outcomes are available, report attrition, and predeclare sensitivity analyses or bounds for missing outcomes; do not silently discard inconvenient participants.

The production release supplies no probability of readiness. Therefore the pilot must not manufacture calibration or false-ready scores from its `competence: null` output. If a separate research predictor is to be evaluated, freeze that predictor and a readiness threshold before the study, use the same predictor in both arms, record predictions before outcomes, and report its calibration/false-ready analysis separately from the policy comparison. Its probabilities would remain research outputs, not production truth.

## Feasibility, sample size and safeguards

Before choosing recruitment targets, obtain independent language review and measure practical completion time, response variability, delayed follow-up feasibility and scorer agreement in an appropriate feasibility process. Then justify the pilot's size from the desired precision or a separately specified power analysis. This document supplies no unsupported numerical sample size or promise of statistical significance.

Obtain informed consent and the applicable institutional review before recruitment. Explain the study, random assignment, optional support, data retention and withdrawal. Use pseudonymous participant references and private scoped event records; keep any contact mapping outside the learner-event payload. Collect only response text required by the rubric, with a declared retention/deletion plan. Do not upload participant responses to an unapproved third party.

Publish the frozen protocol, reviewed material versions, lexical-family assignments, event/scorer schemas, exclusions, and aggregate results where consent and review permit. Report failures, deviations, response burden and negative findings as fully as favorable outcomes. A successful software replay or synthetic experiment does not substitute for this human study.
